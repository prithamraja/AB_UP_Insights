## Claims Processing & Treatment Patterns

**Across Uttar Pradesh, NORMAL discharges account for most paid claim value, but Hamirpur breaks the district-wide pattern by showing a different discharge mix; the same district also departs from the usual LAMA+DAMA concentration.**

1. Statewide, NORMAL discharges account for INR 10.14 crore of paid value, or 85.6% of the INR 11.85 crore total; LIVE contributes INR 86.71 lakh (7.3%), LAMA INR 49.77 lakh (4.2%), DAMA INR 27.41 lakh (2.3%), and DEATH INR 6.85 lakh (0.6%).
2. This NORMAL-led pattern holds in 74 of 75 districts; Hamirpur is the only district that breaks it.
3. A second discharge anomaly appears in the same district set: LAMA and DAMA together dominate the non-normal tail in 74 districts, with only Shamli breaking that concentration.
4. Implication: Hamirpur, and to a lesser extent Shamli, should be checked for discharge coding, referral flow, or patient leave patterns that differ from the rest of UP.

**Among NORMAL discharge cases, pre-authorisation is usually approved or auto-approved, but Mau breaks the pattern and shows a different pre-auth mix.**

1. For NORMAL cases, pre-authorisation is APPROVED in 9,746 cases (49.6%) and AUTO_APPROVED in 6,961 cases (35.4%), versus QUERY_RAISED in 2,357 cases (12.0%) and REJECTED in 586 cases (3.0%).
2. This approval-heavy pattern appears in 74 of 75 districts.
3. Mau is the only district that breaks the expected pre-auth pattern in this group.
4. Implication: Mau’s claim intake or documentation process may be creating a different approval workflow and deserves district-level review.

**NORMAL cases across districts show a broad decline in death trend over time, with Chitrakoot the only district not following the same direction.**

1. Among NORMAL cases, the death trend is decreasing in 74 of 75 districts.
2. Chitrakoot is the lone exception, showing no clear trend in this death measure.
3. The measure itself is very small in absolute terms here, but the direction is still remarkably uniform across the state.
4. Implication: Chitrakoot should be checked separately for small-number volatility or atypical clinical reporting.

**Length of stay is strikingly even across disease categories and divisions, but Chitrakoot is the only district that does not follow the same uniformity on the district-age view.**

1. Across disease categories, average length of stay is tightly clustered: NCD 3.95 days, Maternal-Neonatal 3.93, Communicable 3.89, Surgical 3.87, and Injury 3.70.
2. This near-flat spread is seen in 17 of 18 divisions.
3. Chitrakoot is the only division-level exception in this pattern.
4. Implication: despite different caseload mixes, inpatient stay is highly standardised; Chitrakoot may have outlier service pathways or coding practices.

**Cardiology and Orthopaedics dominate settled package prices across divisions, but Basti breaks the specialty mix while Azamgarh breaks the same pattern under NORMAL discharges.**

1. For settled claims, Cardiology (CARD) contributes INR 3.69 crore, or 40.8% of the total base amount, and Orthopaedics (ORTH) INR 2.07 crore, or 22.9%.
2. This Cardiology-ORTH pair is the top two in 17 of 18 divisions.
3. Basti is the exception in the settled-claim view, while Azamgarh is the exception in the NORMAL-only view.
4. Implication: Basti and Azamgarh may reflect different case mix, specialty availability, or package coding that should be reconciled against facility capability.

**Very large hospitals are not where the highest settled base amount sits; instead, medium and large facilities account for most value, and Cardiology is the only specialty that breaks the very-large hospital pattern.**

1. Large hospitals (101–300 beds) account for INR 30.12 crore, or 40.9% of settled base amount, and medium hospitals for INR 23.94 crore, or 32.5%.
2. Small hospitals contribute INR 14.22 crore (19.3%), while very large hospitals contribute only INR 5.39 crore (7.3%).
3. The expected “very large” alignment holds for 10 of 11 specialties; Cardiology is the exception.
4. Implication: high-value PM-JAY activity is concentrated in mid-sized facilities, so supervision should not focus only on tertiary hospitals.

**Elective claims are concentrated among older adults, but Jhansi breaks the age pattern while the broader division pattern remains anchored on 15–25 years.**

1. For elective admissions, the 60+ age group accounts for INR 33.45 crore, or 38.7% of claimed value, followed by 41–60 at INR 25.96 crore (30.0%).
2. The 15–25 group contributes only INR 7.90 crore, or 9.1%.
3. This 15–25 age pattern is seen in 17 of 18 divisions.
4. Jhansi is the only division that breaks the expected age pattern.
5. Implication: Jhansi may have a different elective-service profile, possibly driven by specialty availability or referral behaviour.

**Settled claims for deaths are almost entirely an elective-treatment phenomenon, with 78 deaths recorded and only Jhansi not matching the district pattern.**

1. In elective admissions, all 78 death cases fall under the DEATH discharge type, representing 100% of the measure.
2. The DEATH pattern is attributed to 17 of 18 divisions.
3. Jhansi is the only division that does not follow this district-level attribution.
4. Implication: death coding in elective cases is highly concentrated and should be checked in Jhansi for clinical routing or discharge classification differences.

**Claim processing is broadly smoother for settled claims over time, with a clear decline in query burden across most divisions, but Basti does not follow the trend.**

1. In settled claims, pre-auth workflow shows 9,746 APPROVED cases (49.6%), 6,961 AUTO_APPROVED (35.4%), 2,357 QUERY_RAISED (12.0%), and 586 REJECTED (3.0%).
2. The same approval-heavy pattern drives 17 of 18 divisions; Basti is the only division without the declining trend.
3. The query burden is not trivial: 2,357 cases still required clarification in the NORMAL-only settled set.
4. Implication: Basti should be reviewed for operational bottlenecks in claim documentation or adjudication.

**By month, claim payments dip sharply in February 2023, but the same low-point pattern is not shared by Settlement TAT or death measures, which makes the February drop look like a payment anomaly rather than a general volume collapse.**

1. The lowest month for paid amount is February 2023 at INR 2.39 lakh, compared with peaks above INR 53.94 lakh in September 2025.
2. Other months such as September 2025 (INR 53.94 lakh), September 2023 (INR 50.18 lakh), and August 2025 (INR 49.89 lakh) are much higher.
3. In the month-wise anomaly view, 9 of 12 measures show the same February 2023 below-pattern, but length of stay, death, and settlement TAT do not.
4. Implication: February 2023 needs a separate check for reporting disruption, billing lag, or a short-term operational shock.

**Settlement turnaround times are very similar across hospital sub-types, but PHCs do not fit the same pattern.**

1. For settled claims, average settlement TAT ranges narrowly from 11.93 days in Neurology (NEURO) to 12.87 days in Obstetrics & Gynaecology (OBGYN/OBG), a spread of only 0.94 days.
2. The even pattern holds across 7 of 8 hospital sub-types.
3. PHCs are the only sub-type that breaks the pattern.
4. Implication: claims processing time is largely standardised, but PHC workflows may need a separate operational lens.

**For NORMAL cases, pre-authorisation in most specialties is approved or auto-approved, but ENT is the only specialty that does not follow that approval-heavy pattern.**

1. Among NORMAL cases, pre-auth is APPROVED in 1,381 cases (51.0%) and AUTO_APPROVED in 928 cases (34.2%), versus QUERY_RAISED in 320 cases (11.8%) and REJECTED in 81 cases (3.0%).
2. This pattern is shared by 10 of 11 specialties.
3. ENT is the only specialty that breaks the approval-heavy pattern.
4. Implication: ENT may have documentation, eligibility, or package-coding issues that generate a different pre-auth experience.

**Among settled claims, pre-auth approvals do not automatically translate into smooth approvals across specialties: most specialties still cluster in QUERY_RAISED and REJECTED for approved amounts, with Paediatrics the exception.**

1. In settled claims, APPROVED pre-auth corresponds to INR 6.08 crore (51.3% of approved amount), AUTO_APPROVED to INR 4.01 crore (33.9%), QUERY_RAISED to INR 1.43 crore (12.0%), and REJECTED to INR 32.89 lakh (2.8%).
2. This “QUERY_RAISED and REJECTED at the tail” pattern is seen in 10 of 11 specialties.
3. Paediatrics (PEDS) is the only specialty that does not follow it.
4. Implication: Paediatrics may have a different approval pathway or coding discipline, and should be reviewed separately.

**Across measures, the top two disease categories are almost always Communicable and NCD, but Length of Stay and Settlement TAT break the otherwise consistent pattern.**

1. In most measures, the leading disease categories are Communicable and NCD, and this holds for 10 of 12 measures.
2. The exceptions are Length of Stay and Settlement TAT, which follow different ranking behaviour.
3. This consistency suggests the same disease mix is driving case counts, claims, approvals, and payments across much of the dataset.
4. Implication: operational variation is more likely to sit in process measures than in disease-mix measures.

**The youngest adult group (15–25) is usually the standout age band across measures, but Length of Stay and Settlement TAT do not follow the same age-ranking pattern.**

1. The 15–25 age band is the standout in 10 of 12 measures.
2. The exceptions are Length of Stay and Settlement TAT, which depart from this age ordering.
3. This shows that age patterns are highly consistent across most claims and payment measures.
4. Implication: youth-heavy activity is a recurring feature of PM-JAY outputs, but process-duration metrics need separate analysis.

**Elective deaths are overwhelmingly recorded as DEATH discharge type in division-level data, and the pattern is almost universal except for Jhansi.**

1. In elective admissions, the death measure is entirely concentrated in DEATH discharge type: 78 cases, or 100%.
2. The pattern holds in 17 of 18 divisions.
3. Jhansi is the only division that does not match the pattern.
4. Implication: this is a strong coding regularity, and any deviation in Jhansi should be audited for discharge classification.

**Seasonal case volume is broadly stable, with a late-summer peak, but Surgical cases do not follow the same 12-month seasonality pattern.**

1. Across disease categories, case counts peak in August 2023 at 818 cases, with other high months in August 2025 at 777 and July 2023 at 776.
2. The seasonality pattern is shared by 4 of 5 disease categories.
3. Surgical cases are the exception.
4. Implication: Surgery demand appears less seasonal than other disease groups and may need different capacity planning.

**In settled claims, emergency admissions are most common in approved pre-auth cases, but Maternal-Neonatal is the only disease category that breaks that pattern.**

1. Among settled claims, APPROVED pre-auth accounts for 209 emergency cases, or 52.4%, followed by AUTO_APPROVED at 125 cases (31.3%).
2. QUERY_RAISED and REJECTED are much smaller at 49 (12.3%) and 16 (4.0%) cases.
3. This pattern holds across 4 of 5 disease categories.
4. Maternal-Neonatal is the only category that breaks the approval pattern.
5. Implication: maternal-neonatal emergencies may be routed differently and deserve separate process monitoring.

**Claim payments by status decline over the year for most statuses, but settled payments do not follow the same monthly pattern.**

1. For amount paid, the highest months include September 2025 at INR 53.94 lakh, September 2023 at INR 50.18 lakh, and August 2025 at INR 49.89 lakh.
2. The low point is February 2023 at INR 2.39 lakh.
3. The declining monthly pattern holds for APPROVED, REJECTED, QUERY_RAISED, and PENDING statuses, but not for SETTLED.
4. Implication: settled disbursement timing is behaving differently from other statuses and may be masking payment-cycle delays.

**Elective admissions in private and public facilities are dominated by Communicable and NCD claims, but CHC is the only hospital sub-type that does not follow that mix.**

1. Under elective admissions, Communicable claims contribute INR 25.21 crore (29.2%) and NCD claims INR 22.70 crore (26.3%).
2. Maternal-Neonatal adds INR 15.62 crore (18.1%), Surgical INR 14.52 crore (16.8%), and Injury INR 8.41 crore (9.7%).
3. This two-category lead is seen in 7 of 8 hospital sub-types.
4. CHC is the exception.
5. Implication: CHCs may be serving a different disease mix and should not be benchmarked identically with other facilities.

**Elective deaths are concentrated in APPROVED claim status across hospital sub-types, with PHC the only facility type that breaks the pattern.**

1. In the elective-death view, APPROVED claim status accounts for 56 cases, or 71.8%, followed by SETTLED at 11 cases (14.1%), QUERY_RAISED at 6 (7.7%), PENDING at 4 (5.1%), and REJECTED at 1 (1.3%).
2. The pattern holds across 7 of 8 hospital sub-types.
3. PHC is the exception.
4. Implication: PHCs may be handling elective death reporting or approval differently and should be reviewed separately.

**Settlement turnaround is almost identical across age bands for elective admissions, but six districts do not follow the same evenness pattern.**

1. Average settlement TAT in elective admissions is tightly grouped: 12.64 days for 15–25, 12.57 for 60+, 12.54 for 41–60, and 12.35 for 26–40.
2. The pattern is even in 69 of 75 districts.
3. Chitrakoot, Firozabad, Kanpur Dehat, Hamirpur, Auraiya, and Etah are the six districts that do not follow the same pattern.
4. Implication: these six districts should be checked for local processing delays or atypical case mix.

**Among NORMAL discharge specialties, pre-auth approval and auto-approval dominate settlement flow, but ONCO is the only specialty that behaves differently on length of stay.**

1. In NORMAL cases, APPROVED pre-auth is 9,746 and AUTO_APPROVED 6,961, together covering 84.9% of the 19,650-case settlement set.
2. QUERY_RAISED remains at 2,357 cases (12.0%) and REJECTED at 586 (3.0%).
3. This even approval pattern is seen in 10 of 11 specialties.
4. Oncology (ONCO) is the only specialty that does not follow the same length-of-stay evenness.
5. Implication: ONCO may have distinct clinical pathways or coding conventions that warrant separate operational tracking.

**The month-2023-02 outlier shows up across most metrics, but Length of Stay, Death, and Settlement TAT are not affected in the same way, suggesting a payment or reporting issue rather than a clinical one.**

1. The February 2023 low point appears in 9 of 12 measures, including case_count, amount_claimed, amount_approved, amount_paid, is_emergency, is_lama_dama, query_count, base_amount, and computed_final_amount.
2. Length of Stay, death, and settlement TAT do not share the same outlier behaviour.
3. That makes the anomaly look process-related rather than patient-care related.
4. Implication: February 2023 should be tested for system downtime, late posting, or reporting lag.

**Among settled claims, Injury is the smallest disease category by bed-size distribution, but the very-large-hospital bucket is the one that does not fit the overall pattern.**

1. For settled case_count by bed size, Communicable leads with 897 cases (30.4%), followed by NCD at 718 (24.4%) and Maternal-Neonatal at 527 (17.9%).
2. Surgical contributes 522 cases (17.7%), while Injury is smallest at 283 cases (9.6%).
3. The injury-led pattern appears in 3 of 4 bed-size buckets.
4. Very-large hospitals (300+) are the exception.
5. Implication: larger hospitals may be less injury-heavy than expected and should be benchmarked separately from the rest of the network.

## District-Level Monthly Performance Trends

**Cards issued show a strong monthly seasonality in 74 of 75 districts, but Mahoba shifts to a different period, making its card flow the clear outlier.**

1. Across 74 of 75 districts, `cards_issued` follows the same monthly seasonal pattern, with the dominant period in `PERIOD_3`.
2. The statewide monthly total reached 195,619 cards across 50 months, with the heaviest months in 2023-07 (7,345), 2023-08 (7,342), 2023-05 (7,341), 2023-06 (7,267), and 2023-03 (7,205).
3. Mahoba is the only district that breaks this pattern, showing `PERIOD_4` instead of `PERIOD_3`.
4. Bottom-line: card issuance is broadly synchronized across UP, but Mahoba needs review for timing differences in outreach, data entry, or campaign execution.

**Claims utilisation per 1,000 beneficiaries jumped in 2023 across 74 districts, but Balrampur shifted the timing to 2023Q2 instead of the usual 2023Q1 change point.**

1. In 74 of 75 districts, `claims_per_1000_beneficiaries` shows a quarter-level change point at `2023Q1`.
2. The statewide average peaked at 7.52 in 2023Q3, after 7.29 in 2023Q2 and 3.99 in 2024Q1, compared with 0.0 in 2022Q2 and 2022Q4.
3. The standard shift is visible at 2023Q1 with an average of 3.2, but Balrampur moved later to `2023Q2`.
4. Implication: utilisation accelerated sharply in 2023, and Balrampur’s delayed turning point may indicate a later service ramp-up or reporting lag.

**Division-level payment volume rose through most of the period, but Prayagraj behaved differently from the rest of the state.**

1. `payment_count` shows an increasing monthly trend in 17 of 18 divisions.
2. The statewide monthly total peaked at 110 in 2025-09, followed by 108 in 2025-03, 102 in 2023-08, 101 in 2026-01, and 100 in 2025-10.
3. The pattern is consistent across all major divisions listed, but Prayagraj is the only division flagged as a type change rather than a clean increase.
4. Implication: payment throughput is generally rising, but Prayagraj should be checked for a different mix of transactions or a structural data issue.

**Average claim amounts are broadly even across all 18 divisions, but Moradabad and Chitrakoot do not fit the same evenness pattern.**

1. `avg_claim_amount` is classified as even across 16 of 18 divisions.
2. The division-level range is fairly tight overall: Hamirpur is highest at INR 60,068.28, while Maharajganj is lowest at INR 36,769.37.
3. Most divisions sit in a narrow band around INR 37,000–INR 60,000, which is why the pattern is marked even.
4. Moradabad and Chitrakoot are the two divisions that do not follow this same evenness profile.
5. Implication: average claim value is stable across UP, but the two exceptions merit review for case-mix, package mix, or billing behaviour.

**District-level claims utilisation peaked in 2023 for most districts, but Bareilly, Basti, Deoria, Etah, Etawah, Kaushambi, Lakhimpur Kheri, Mathura, Pilibhit, and Sambhal do not follow that same peak year.**

1. `claims_per_1000_beneficiaries` by year peaks in 2023 for 65 of 75 districts.
2. The statewide average is highest in 2023 at 5.69, then falls to 3.77 in 2024, 3.14 in 2025, and 1.58 in 2026.
3. Ten districts do not share that 2023 peak pattern: Bareilly, Basti, Deoria, Etah, Etawah, Kaushambi, Lakhimpur Kheri, Mathura, Pilibhit, and Sambhal.
4. Implication: utilisation momentum was strongest in 2023, but the ten outlier districts likely need closer review for delayed adoption or different service patterns.

**Across the state, enrolment and card issuance fall after 2023Q2, but a few districts peak later than the norm.**

1. At district-quarter level, `new_beneficiaries` shows a decreasing trend in 65 of 75 districts, and `new_households` follows the same downward pattern in most districts.
2. Statewide `new_beneficiaries` peaks in 2023Q2 at 22,807, then stays slightly lower in 2023Q3 at 22,642 before dropping sharply to 454 in 2025Q4 and 2 in 2026Q1.
3. For `new_households`, the state peaks in 2023Q2 at 21,394 and remains high in 2023Q3 at 21,173, but several districts shift their peak to 2023Q3 instead.
4. The main exceptions are Ayodhya, Banda, Etah, Gautam Buddha Nagar, Hapur, Kaushambi, Lucknow, Moradabad, Raebareli, and Unnao for `new_beneficiaries`, and Agra, Azamgarh, Chitrakoot, Varanasi, Gorakhpur, Prayagraj, and Mirzapur for `new_households`.
5. Implication: the statewide enrolment surge has weakened over time, and the late-peak districts should be checked for campaign timing, saturation, or reporting gaps.

**New beneficiary enrolment and new household enrolment both peak in 2023Q2 in most divisions, but several divisions shift to a later quarter.**

1. At division level, `new_beneficiaries` and `new_households` both show the same seasonal peak around `PERIOD_3`, with `new_beneficiaries` peaking in 2023Q2 at 22,807 and `new_households` at 21,394.

4. Implication: district mobilization was concentrated in mid-2023, but the later-peaking divisions may reflect staggered outreach or delayed data capture.

1. ## Hospital Infrastructure & Specialty Capacity

**Across Uttar Pradesh, Cardiology (CARD) and Orthopaedics (ORTH) dominate claim value in almost every district and bed-size group, with Shamli the only district where Obstetrics & Gynaecology (OBG) enters the top two.**

1. Statewide claim value totals INR 100.27 crore, of which Cardiology contributes INR 40.04 crore (39.9%) and Orthopaedics INR 25.78 crore (25.7%); the next highest specialties are Obstetrics & Gynaecology at INR 8.49 crore and General Surgery (GS) at INR 7.22 crore.
2. This top-two pattern holds in 74 of 75 districts (98.7%); Shamli is the only exception, where the top two shift from Cardiology and Orthopaedics to Cardiology and OBG.
3. The same Cardiology-Orthopaedics concentration also appears across bed-size groups, where Cardiology alone remains the standout specialty and the smaller hospitals do not change the overall hierarchy.
4. Implication: if claims oversight is focused on outliers, Shamli should be reviewed first for specialty mix, referral pathways, or coding differences because it is the only district breaking the statewide spending pattern.

**Hospital clinicians’ average experience is remarkably uniform across divisions, but Agra is the one division that does not follow the same even profile.**

1. Across divisions, average clinical experience ranges narrowly from 16.58 years in Burns (BURNS) to 18.32 years in Paediatrics (PEDS) and Oncology (ONCO), with the overall average at 270.14 across the specialty breakdown shown.
2. The evenness pattern holds in 17 of 18 divisions (94.4%); Agra is the only division that deviates from the otherwise flat distribution.
3. The spread is small: the top specialties cluster around 18.25–18.32 years, while the lower end still stays near 17.48–16.58 years.
4. Implication: Agra’s deviation is worth checking for staffing mix or reporting differences, since the rest of the state shows little variation in clinician experience by specialty.

**Small hospitals are the clear utilisation outlier in division-level case load, and Jhansi is the only division that does not fit that small-hospital pattern.**

1. For cases per bed, small hospitals (<=30 beds) account for 0.20 cases per bed, versus 0.05 in medium hospitals, 0.02 in large hospitals, and 0.01 in very large hospitals.
2. This small-hospital utilisation pattern appears in 17 of 18 divisions (94.4%); Jhansi is the exception.
3. The same pattern is also visible in the division headline view for small hospitals, where the small-bed group remains the dominant utilisation category almost everywhere.
4. Implication: Jhansi should be checked for atypical patient routing, bed designation, or case recording because its utilisation profile breaks the state’s usual small-hospital concentration.
